      How it's scored

      bide is independent: it uses the timetable and the bus's own reported progress. bide+ blends BC Transit's prediction, bide's and the timetable, with weights for each lead time learned on an earlier day. Both notice when the feed's clock is a whole hour out and correct it. Settings are always learned on a previous day, never the day being scored.

      What you're looking at

      A live 3D map of Greater Victoria. Everything marked live comes from a public feed and refreshes on its own; the time it updates is noted.

      Buses live · every 10 s

      A bus, drawn where BC Transit's GPS says it is, gliding between reports. The white arrow on its roof points the way it's going.
      Its colour shows lateness, crowding or route: pick with Colour buses by. Grey means BC Transit has sent no prediction for that trip.
      A slow ring under a bus means it's stopped at a stop.
      A dashed amber ring: the bus is bunched, running within 350 m of another bus on the same route and direction. Riders behind them wait twice as long, then two arrive together. The count is in the bottom-left card; click it to visit each pair.
      Tap a bus to light up its route ahead and its next four stops, with BC Transit's arrival time beside bide's own.
      Bus stops appear as small dots once you're down at street level. Tap one for its departures board: every bus due in the next 75 minutes, BC Transit's time beside bide's. "+2" means bide expects it two minutes later than BC Transit says.

      The scoreboard every morning

      Every BC Transit arrival prediction is recorded and later checked against when the bus actually arrived. bide's two models answer the same questions using only what was known at the time: bide works from the timetable and the bus's own progress; bide+ blends that with BC Transit's figure, with weights learned on earlier days.

      The city

      Terracotta lines: how many buses an hour are scheduled on each street at the time on the time bar. Thicker means more. From the timetable, not live.
      Buildings rise as you zoom in (OpenStreetMap). Lit windows at night are for atmosphere, not real occupancy.
      Street names appear once you're close.

      Live conditions

      Pale drifting wisps: the wind right now across 20 points of the region (Open-Meteo, every 15 min). Faster and brighter means stronger. Seen only from city height.
      The sea gets rougher with the real wave height off Dallas Road, with whitecaps above about a metre (Open-Meteo marine, every 30 min).
      Rippling rings: earthquakes in the past week within 250 km (USGS). Coral is the last day; rings on the horizon are beyond the map, in their true direction. Hover one for details.
      Striped stretches of road: amber is road work, red an incident (DriveBC, every 5 min).
      Tinted patches with rings: power outages (BC Hydro, every 2 min).
      Paper pins: local headlines that name a neighbourhood (CHEK, Times Colonist, Victoria News, Victoria Buzz).
      A coloured banner at the top: a weather warning from Environment Canada in force for Greater Victoria.

      Not live

      Harbour life: the floatplanes, harbour ferries, the Coho, sailboats and gulls are animation, there to make the harbour feel lived in. Free aircraft and ship-tracking feeds don't allow it.

      The time bar

      Drag it to see the light and the timetable at any hour of today; press ▶ to play the day. Buses always show where they are right now. Live brings everything back to the present.
